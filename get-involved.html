Run for Local OfficeHow to get started with elections and candidate paperwork
  1. Choose the position. Start with Norwood Elections & Voting and the town government guide. Norwood uses representative Town Meeting; Town Meeting members run from their precincts.
  2. Contact the Town Clerk early. Ask which seats will appear on the next Annual Town Election ballot, whether you are eligible, how to obtain official nomination papers, how many certified voter signatures are required, and the specific collection and filing deadlines. Requirements depend on the office and election year.
  3. Check your registration and precinct. Confirm your voter registration, address and precinct before collecting signatures. Precinct-specific seats have different eligibility and signature rules from townwide offices.
  4. Collect signatures on official papers. Use the paperwork and instructions supplied by election officials; allow time for signatures to be verified. Do not rely on an older year's filing dates.
  5. File on time and review campaign rules. Check candidate acceptance, reporting, financial disclosures and any applicable campaign-finance requirements with the Town Clerk and Massachusetts Office of Campaign and Political Finance.
  6. Meet voters and participate. Attend public meetings, review current town issues, and share accurate information about your candidacy. For appointed boards, ask about the Town's current Talent Bank process instead.

Town Meeting seats: Massachusetts' Citizen’s Guide to Town Meetings explains representative Town Meeting elections. Members are elected by precinct, and the process begins before Town Meeting itself—not by signing up on meeting night.
