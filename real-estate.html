📍Everything Close ByTown center, restaurants, shopping, services, recreation and regional access.›

Norwood combines the convenience of a Boston suburb with a strong community of its own. Situated between Boston and Providence, the town has three commuter rail stations and convenient access to Route 1, Route 1A and I-95/Route 128, making it easy to reach communities throughout Greater Boston and the region.

At the same time, there is plenty close at hand. Norwood Center brings everyday needs, food, shopping, recreation and community life close together, with a lively town center and Town Common, a wide range of restaurants, local shops, grocery stores and everyday services, arts, entertainment and community events, and parks, trails and recreation. Major shopping destinations and hundreds of additional restaurants, stores and services are a short distance away.

🏫Schools & EducationPublic schools, early childhood, private and vocational options, and higher education nearby.›

Norwood Public Schools serves students from pre-K through 12, with six elementary schools, a middle school, and Norwood High School. Students also have access to vocational and agricultural education through Blue Hills and Norfolk Aggie. The district offers a range of advanced coursework, including a solid AP lineup, alongside comprehensive special education services and supports. Outside the classroom, students participate in athletics, music and performing arts, academic organizations, and a wide variety of clubs. Norwood’s music community is supported by an active Parents Music Association that helps expand opportunities for student musicians. And individual schools have earned broader recognition, like Prescott Elementary being named a National Blue Ribbon School in 2019. Together, those offerings give students many ways to find their fit and grow.

Why We Chose Norwood for Our Family

When my wife and I were deciding where to raise our family, the schools mattered enormously to us. But test scores weren’t the whole story.

I have a Ph.D. in clinical psychology, and my wife has a master’s degree in social work. We both understood that aggregate school test scores reflect much more than what happens inside a classroom. Parental education, family income, language, access to resources, and opportunities outside of school all have strong relationships with academic outcomes.

I grew up in Westwood and received an excellent education there. I didn’t assume that a higher average test score in Westwood or another affluent community meant that our children would receive a proportionately better education simply by living there.

What appealed to us about Norwood was the combination: strong schools, a diverse community, a real town center, convenient transportation, parks and recreation, and a community where our children would grow up alongside people from a wider range of backgrounds and experiences.

Our children are thriving in Norwood Public Schools. They have had teachers who know them, opportunities in academics, music, activities and athletics, and a school community in which they have been able to grow into themselves.

We were also impressed by the care and professionalism the schools showed during COVID-19, an extraordinarily difficult period for students, families and educators. That experience reinforced for us that a school system is more than a set of numbers.

Norwood also offered something that mattered to us as a family: value. We could put down roots in a community we genuinely liked without feeling that we had to pay a premium simply for a town name or a higher average test score.

We chose Norwood. And knowing what we know now, we would choose it again.

🚆Commuting & TransitCommuter rail, 34E bus and commute context.›

Norwood is served by three Franklin/Foxboro Line commuter-rail stations — Norwood Depot, Norwood Central and Windsor Gardens — plus MBTA Route 34E bus service, which travels through Norwood on Washington Street, connecting Norwood to Forest Hills Station to the north and Walpole Center to the south. Route 1, Route 1A and I-95/Route 128 also run through or directly serve Norwood, giving residents several ways to reach Greater Boston and the wider region.

🤝Community & CulturePeople, languages, organizations and places to connect.›

Norwood has a civic and community life that extends well beyond housing, with community organizations, volunteer opportunities, cultural and faith communities, town institutions and events throughout the year.

Norwood’s population was estimated at 31,714 in 2025. Based on the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 American Community Survey, 19.8% of residents are foreign-born and 24.8% of people age 5 and older speak a language other than English at home. About 20.0% of residents are under 18 and 17.6% are age 65 or older.

The same Census data report Norwood as 70.7% White alone, 7.1% Black alone, 6.7% Asian alone, and 9.9% two or more races; 10.0% of residents identify as Hispanic or Latino of any race. The town’s school-age population is notably more diverse than the population as a whole: in 2025–26, Norwood Public Schools reported 53.2% White, 13.2% Black, 22.7% Hispanic or Latino, 6.2% Asian, and 4.0% multiracial enrollment. The median household income for 2020–2024 was $107,030, and 55.4% of adults age 25 and older held a bachelor’s degree or higher.U.S. Census Bureau

💼Employment & EconomyLocal employers plus access to the Boston and Providence job markets.›

Norwood has a diverse local economy, with employers ranging from major national and international companies to health care and service organizations, manufacturers, restaurants, retailers and locally owned businesses. Major employment and commercial activity is concentrated along Route 1 and University Avenue, with additional businesses and services in Norwood Center and throughout town.

🛡️Crime & Public SafetyCrime statistics, community policing and local public-safety context.›

Norwood has a strong tradition of community policing, with officers regularly getting out of their cruisers and walking neighborhoods, business districts and schools. The department has emphasized this kind of direct community contact for decades; in 2023 alone, officers conducted more than 4,000 foot patrols throughout town.Sources
Norwood Police Department 2023 Annual Report; Norwood Historical Society archive on the department’s community-policing initiative.2023 Annual Report ↗Community-policing history ↗

That emphasis on approachability also shows up in small ways. Norwood officers carry trading cards featuring themselves, and local children often try to collect the full set. Mustang Sally, the department’s Golden Retriever therapy and community resource dog, has her own especially popular cards as well.Source
WBZ-TV / CBS Boston, September 16, 2024.Open source ↗

Officer Geoffrey Baguma and Mustang Sally regularly work in the schools and at community events, adding another visible community-engagement presence for the department.
