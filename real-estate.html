📍Everything Close ByTown center, restaurants, shopping, services, recreation and regional access.›

Norwood combines the convenience of a Boston suburb with a strong community of its own. Situated between Boston and Providence, the town has three commuter rail stations and convenient access to Route 1, Route 1A and I-95/Route 128, making it easy to reach communities throughout Greater Boston and the region.

At the same time, there is plenty close at hand. Norwood Center brings everyday needs, food, shopping, recreation and community life close together, with a lively town center and Town Common, a wide range of restaurants, local shops, grocery stores and everyday services, arts, entertainment and community events, and parks, trails and recreation. Major shopping destinations and hundreds of additional restaurants, stores and services are a short distance away.

🏫Schools & EducationPublic schools, early childhood, private and vocational options, and higher education nearby.›

Norwood Public Schools includes Little Mustangs Preschool Academy, Willett Early Childhood Center, five elementary schools, Coakley Middle School and Norwood High School. Little Mustangs is an integrated preschool program for Norwood children ages 3 and 4, including peer-role-model placements and services for eligible children with disabilities.Source 1
Norwood Public Schools — Little Mustangs Preschool AcademyOpen source ↗

Public-school choices are only part of the picture. Norwood is also home to Saint Catherine of Siena School, a Catholic preschool-through-grade-8 schoolSource 2
Saint Catherine of Siena School — AboutOpen source ↗, and families have additional private and parochial options in nearby communities. For career and technical education, Norwood is one of the nine member communities of Blue Hills Regional Technical School in Canton; eligible rising or current 8th- and 9th-grade Norwood students may apply for admission.Source 3
Blue Hills Regional Technical School — Admissions PolicyOpen source ↗

Higher education is close at hand as well. Norwood itself is home to FINE Mortuary College at 150 Kerry Place, which offers an accredited Associate in Applied Science degree in Funeral Service.Source 4
FINE Mortuary College — ABFSE accreditation informationOpen source ↗ And despite the name, there is no university on University Avenue — but living in Norwood places students within reach of the large concentration of colleges and universities across both the Boston and Providence metropolitan areas.

School rankings and test scores can be useful context, but they are not the whole story. This guide emphasizes official state and district information alongside programs, opportunities and the practical choices available to families.

🚆Commuting & TransitCommuter rail, 34E bus and commute context.›

Norwood is served by three Franklin/Foxboro Line commuter-rail stations — Norwood Depot, Norwood Central and Windsor Gardens — plus MBTA Route 34E bus service. Route 1, Route 1A and I-95/Route 128 also run through or directly serve Norwood, giving residents several ways to reach Greater Boston and the wider region.

🤝Community & CulturePeople, languages, organizations and places to connect.›

Norwood has a civic and community life that extends well beyond housing, with community organizations, volunteer opportunities, cultural and faith communities, town institutions and events throughout the year.

Norwood’s population was estimated at 31,714 in 2025. Based on the U.S. Census Bureau’s 2020–2024 American Community Survey, 19.8% of residents are foreign-born and 24.8% of people age 5 and older speak a language other than English at home. About 20.0% of residents are under 18 and 17.6% are age 65 or older.

The same Census data report Norwood as 70.7% White alone, 7.1% Black alone, 6.7% Asian alone, and 9.9% two or more races; 10.0% of residents identify as Hispanic or Latino of any race. The median household income for 2020–2024 was $107,030, and 55.4% of adults age 25 and older held a bachelor’s degree or higher.U.S. Census Bureau
QuickFacts — Norwood town, Norfolk County, Massachusetts; 2020–2024 ACS and 2025 population estimate.Open source ↗

The town also has a varied religious landscape, with Christian, Jewish, Muslim, Hindu and Buddhist communities and resources in Norwood and nearby communities.

⚡Utilities & Everyday CostsElectricity, water/sewer, internet, trash & recycling — with nearby-town comparisons.›

Norwood’s municipal services can affect the real cost of living in town. Norwood Light is the Town’s community-owned electric utility and also operates Norwood Light Broadband. Its newer Broadband Fiber service uses a 100% fiber-optic network with symmetrical speeds; the fiber-to-the-home upgrade is still being completed, so availability should be checked by address.

Norwood also provides free public Wi-Fi on the Town Common, a service launched in partnership with Norwood Light.

Norwood receives MWRA water from the protected Quabbin and Wachusett reservoir system and distributes it through the Town’s locally maintained water system. The latest annual report covers 2025 drinking-water testing and was published in June 2026.

💼Employment & EconomyLocal employers plus access to the Boston and Providence job markets.›

Norwood has a diverse local economy, with employers ranging from major national and international companies to health care and service organizations, manufacturers, restaurants, retailers and locally owned businesses. Major employment and commercial activity is concentrated along Route 1 and University Avenue, with additional businesses and services in Norwood Center and throughout town.

Among the larger corporate presences, Moderna operates major manufacturing and development facilities in NorwoodSource 6
Moderna — Manufacturing: Why Norwood?Open source ↗, while FM maintains a major Norwood office. Those sit alongside regional employers, medical and service organizations, manufacturers and the many smaller businesses that make up the everyday local economy.

Norwood’s location adds another dimension. U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics payroll data for July 2026 show about 2.77 million nonfarm jobs in the Boston-Cambridge-Newton metropolitan area and about 744,500 in Providence-Warwick.Source 5
U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — July 2026 metropolitan payroll employmentOpen source ↗ Access to two large and diverse employment markets can provide flexibility for career advancement and career transitions without necessarily requiring a household move. For two-career households, it also broadens the geography in which both people can consider employment.

🛡️Crime & Public SafetyCrime statistics, community policing and local public-safety context.›

Norwood has a strong tradition of community policing, with officers regularly getting out of their cruisers and walking neighborhoods, business districts and schools. The department has emphasized this kind of direct community contact for decades; in 2023 alone, officers conducted more than 4,000 foot patrols throughout town.Sources
Norwood Police Department 2023 Annual Report; Norwood Historical Society archive on the department’s community-policing initiative.2023 Annual Report ↗Community-policing history ↗

That emphasis on approachability also shows up in small ways. Norwood officers carry trading cards featuring themselves, and local children often try to collect the full set. Mustang Sally, the department’s Golden Retriever therapy and community resource dog, has her own especially popular cards as well.Source
WBZ-TV / CBS Boston, September 16, 2024.Open source ↗

Officer Geoffrey Baguma and Mustang Sally regularly work in the schools and at community events, adding another visible community-engagement presence for the department.
